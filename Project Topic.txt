Projektbeschreibung
In this project a 3D object detection model shall be trained and evaluated using the experiment data from a multi-perspective LIDAR recording dataset in an underground parking. The goal is to assess how a pre-trained detection model can be fine-tuned on the two-sensor experiment data and to quantify the benefits of multi-sensor fusion on the evaluation data. The target classes are car, bicycle, and person.

Project approach:

Data source: A two-sensor LiDAR experiment dataset featuring identical scenes captured from two distinct viewpoints, plus a merged multi-view configuration.

Model: Use an existing, pre-trained 3D object detection model (e.g., PointPillars/SECOND or CenterPoint variants) with open pre-trained weights.

Training strategy: Fine-tune the pre-trained model exclusively on the provided experiment data. Do not train from scratch and do not incorporate other datasets for training.

Preprocessing:

Converting point clouds to the model’s preferred representation (e.g., voxelization for PointPillars).
Handling two-sensor data and the merged data as separate inputs to evaluate single-sensor vs. merged performance.
Optional noise reduction or ceiling removal if consistent with the data pipeline.
Evaluation:

Metrics: Mean Average Precision (mAP) across IoU thresholds (e.g., AP30/40/50/60 and overall mAP), per-class AP, and comparisons between single-sensor and merged configurations.
Inference speed: report approximate per-frame inference time and data-merge time for the evaluation setup.
Qualitative analysis: inspect detections to illustrate typical successes and failure modes.
